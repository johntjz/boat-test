Phase 0: Software-in-the-Loop (SIL) Validation (Months 1–9 | TRL 3 → 4)

Digital Twin & Algorithmic De-Risking

Before committing significant capital to physical vessel fabrication, we prioritize a Software-First Development Strategy. By establishing a high-fidelity 6-DOF Digital Twin and HIL benchtop within HKSTP, we mathematically validate the core flight control algorithms first—eliminating physical crash and asset-loss risks during early-stage control loop tuning.

1. Model-in-the-Loop (MIL)

6-DOF hydrodynamic physics engine in Gazebo/MATLAB simulating hull wave-making resistance, T-foil lift/drag, cavitation limits, and surface-proximity decay.

2. Software-in-the-Loop (SIL)

Real-time C++/RTOS flight control algorithms tested against virtual Victoria Harbour & GBA wave states (JONSWAP models) to prove 100 Hz control stability.

3. Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL)

Flashing validated software onto target Jetson/STM32 compute nodes connected to benchtop waterproof flap actuators ensuring sub-8ms deterministic latency.

Phase 1: Sub-Scale Technology Validation (Months 6–14 | TRL 5 → 6)

BAYWING-D1 (1:8 Scale Autonomous Testbed)

Following mathematical proof in Phase 0, we move to physical validation. High-speed hydrofoiling is fundamentally an aerospace control problem. Because dynamic instability scales inversely with physical size, a sub-scale 3.56-meter vessel experiences higher natural oscillation frequencies than the full commercial crafts. Stabilizing the D1 in real-world harbor sea-states completely de-risks the physical flight control stack.

Phase 2: Commercial Transition & Revenue (Months 12–24 | TRL 7 → 8)

BAYWING-D2 (25-Pax Commercial Pilot)

Jumping directly from a 3.5-meter drone to a 28.5-meter 150-pax ferry is un-fundable by early-stage VCs. The BAYWING-D2 is our commercial bridge: a 15m, 25–40 passenger vessel designed to validate human-rated intra-city Hong Kong transit, secure HKMD marine certifications, and generate recurring revenue. Critically, our Sheung Wan Landing No. 1 shore-power node (in active technical review with HK Electric's Distribution Planning Department for a 250–900 kW LV supply + BESS buffer) serves as the dual-purpose validation hub for both the D2's 500 kW fast charger and the EF-150's first BESS buffer module.

Bridging Sheung Wan to Intra-Harbour Piers

While the D1 de-risks our flight-control IP at near-zero OPEX in Tolo Harbour, the D2 targets short-haul intra-harbour & island routes (Central Piers 7–10, Wan Chai, Discovery Bay). By establishing our first 300–900 kW LV shore-power & BESS demarcation node at Sheung Wan Landing No. 1 (a public landing steps from the Central and Macau terminals), we validate both the D2's 500 kW fast-charging operations and the first modular BESS unit for the EF-150 Macau corridor[cite: 1].

The VC "Gap" Funding Hook

The D2 bridges the "Valley of Death." This staged capital architecture minimizes founder and early angel dilution by leveraging non-dilutive statutory capital across both prototyping and pilot phases. Pitching VCs to fund a HK$ 10M (~US$ 1.28M) Seed/Pre-A round directly triggers the government's ITF Enterprise Support Scheme (ESS) 1:1 matching grant, immediately doubling the capital to HK$ 20M (~US$ 2.56M) to fully fund the D2 construction, testing, and launch.
