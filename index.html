Phase 0: Software-in-the-Loop (SIL) Validation (Months 1–9 | TRL 3 → 4)

Digital Twin & Algorithmic De-Risking

Before committing significant capital to physical vessel fabrication, we prioritize a Software-First Development Strategy. By establishing a high-fidelity 6-DOF Digital Twin and HIL benchtop within HKSTP, we mathematically validate the core flight control algorithms first—eliminating physical crash and asset-loss risks during early-stage control loop tuning.

1. Model-in-the-Loop (MIL)

6-DOF hydrodynamic physics engine in Gazebo/MATLAB simulating hull wave-making resistance, T-foil lift/drag, cavitation limits, and surface-proximity decay.

2. Software-in-the-Loop (SIL)

Real-time C++/RTOS flight control algorithms tested against virtual Victoria Harbour & GBA wave states (JONSWAP models) to prove 100 Hz control stability.

3. Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL)

Flashing validated software onto target Jetson/STM32 compute nodes connected to benchtop waterproof flap actuators ensuring sub-8ms deterministic latency.

Phase 1: Sub-Scale Technology Validation (Months 6–14 | TRL 5 → 6)

BAYWING-D1 (1:8 Scale Autonomous Testbed)

Following mathematical proof in Phase 0, we move to physical validation. High-speed hydrofoiling is fundamentally an aerospace control problem. Because dynamic instability scales inversely with physical size, a sub-scale 3.56-meter vessel experiences higher natural oscillation frequencies than the full commercial crafts. Stabilizing the D1 in real-world harbor sea-states completely de-risks the physical flight control stack.
